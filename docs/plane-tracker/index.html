An outage is not an attribution

Direct FAA evidence: ATCSCC Advisory 117, dated November 13 at 21:21 UTC, attributes extended flight-plan retention across en-route centers to a nationwide communications failure. This establishes the reported operational failure, not its underlying cause or a cyberattack. FAA operational advisory feed (rolling page; its contents change).

Established in the reviewed sources

A nationwide departure restriction was issued after communication problems at air-traffic-control facilities.

Still unresolved

The failed component, its underlying failure mechanism, and whether malicious activity was involved.

A useful investigation lead

If simultaneous failures at different facilities are confirmed, examine their shared communication infrastructure and recent changes. This is a hypothesis, not a diagnosed cause.

Not established

A connection to the Microsoft outage, an AI deployment or the New Orleans vessel collision. Timing and social-feed proximity alone do not connect incidents.

How claims are checked

Keep event time, publication time and retrieval time separate. Follow quotations back to the original source. Reposts of one report are not independent witnesses. Require incident-specific evidence before identifying a cause or attacker; retain corrections instead of silently rewriting the record.

Public flight maps show aircraft observations and operational effects. They do not reveal whether a communications failure was deliberate. Missing tracking coverage is not proof of an empty sky.

Source and access limitations

The flight count and official-statement timeline come from Flightradar24’s incident publication. The early controller report is an unverified lead supplied as pasted X text. Direct X access was unavailable during preparation; the official statement was read as reproduced by reporting. No raw controller recording, network telemetry or forensic evidence was inspected.

No claim is made that every control center failed in the same way: the FAA’s reported statement refers to multiple centers, while Duffy’s quoted statement refers to all centers.
